Custom HID Taiko Game Controller

A custom game controller based on the RP2040 microcontroller that translates physical drum strikes detected by piezoelectric shock sensors into digital USB-HID inputs for high-precision rhythm gameplay.

Why I Built This

Commercial Taiko controllers are expensive and lack customization for competitive play. I wanted to design one from scratch to get hands-on experience with the full hardware lifecycle — from selecting a microcontroller and designing input-protection circuits around real-world sensor behavior, to routing a USB-compliant PCB and preparing it for fabrication. The project forced me to solve real analog-to-digital challenges: conditioning unpredictable piezo spikes into clean ADC reads, and ensuring the USB differential pairs met impedance targets for reliable HID communication.

Hardware Summary

A purpose-built USB game controller that converts analog piezoelectric drum strikes into low-latency digital inputs. The design pairs an RP2040 MCU with a robust input-protection front-end and a USB-C HID interface, all captured in a compact 2-layer PCB designed for reliable fabrication.

MCU RP2040
Interface USB-C HID
Sensors 4× Piezo ADC
EDA Tool Altium Designer
Status In Fabrication

Hardware Architecture & Schematic Design

  • Microcontroller: Integrated an RP2040 with external QSPI flash memory.
  • Input Protection: Conditioned high-voltage piezo spikes using 3.3V Zener diodes and 1MΩ pull-down resistors to protect the ADC pins.
  • Power & Interface: Implemented USB-C (with 5.1kΩ CC pull-downs) and regulated 5V to 3.3V via an AP2112K LDO.

PCB Layout & DFM (Altium Designer)

  • Signal Integrity: Routed impedance-controlled USB differential pairs (D+/D-) to ensure signal integrity between the MCU and the host PC.
  • Power Delivery: Minimized parasitic inductance by clustering decoupling capacitors directly beneath the RP2040.
  • Manufacturing: Cleared complex DRCs and tailored component footprints to ensure reliable manufacturing.

Next Steps

  • Component assembly and stencil-assisted SMD reflow
  • Bootloader flashing and bring-up testing
  • Developing interrupt-driven C/C++ firmware for the USB-HID stack

Mixed-Signal Oscilloscope

A 2-channel, mixed-signal data acquisition tool designed to capture, condition, and digitize high-speed electrical signals. Built around the STM32G474RET6 for its specialized mixed-signal architecture and high-resolution internal ADCs.

Why I Built This

I wanted to tackle the hardest problems in mixed-signal board design: keeping analog measurement accuracy while a digital MCU and switching power supply share the same PCB. Building an oscilloscope from scratch meant I had to design a true analog front-end with relay-switched ranges and precision op-amp buffering, implement bipolar power supplies isolated from the noisy digital rail, and partition the ground plane with star-point grounding — all decisions that textbooks describe but only become real when you route the board yourself and see whether the ADC reads clean.

Hardware Summary

A from-scratch oscilloscope built on the STM32G4 platform, featuring a high-bandwidth analog front-end with relay-switched input ranges, precision bipolar power supplies, and DMA-driven interleaved ADC sampling at 2 MSps. The mixed-signal PCB layout enforces strict analog/digital ground isolation via star-point grounding to minimize noise coupling.

MCU STM32G474RET6
Channels 2-Channel
Sample Rate 2 MSps
Power ±3.3V Bipolar
Status In Fabrication

Hardware Architecture & Schematic Design

  • Analog Front-End (AFE): Designed a high-bandwidth input chain (BNC, TQ2SA relay, TMUX1108) with OPA356 buffering and scaling.
  • Input Protection: Implemented BAT54S clamping diodes to shunt over-voltage events and protect the STM32's ADC pins.
  • Power Supply: Built a dedicated ±3.3V bipolar supply (LM27762) isolated from the main 5V buck converter for clean AC signal biasing.
  • Data Acquisition: Leveraged the STM32G4's hardware for 2 MSps interleaved, DMA-driven ADC sampling.

Mixed-Signal Layout & DFM (Altium Designer)

  • Strict Signal Isolation: Prevented ground loops by partitioning analog and digital ground zones, joined only at a single star point.
  • Power Delivery: Minimized parasitic inductance and loop area by clustering decoupling capacitors directly beneath the STM32 on the bottom copper layer.
  • DFM: Passed rigorous DRCs, optimized thermal polygon pours, and applied clear silkscreen labeling.

Next Steps

  • Stencil-assisted SMD reflow assembly
  • Developing C/C++ firmware to configure DMA pipelines and ADC triggering logic
